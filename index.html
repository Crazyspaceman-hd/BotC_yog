🔬 About this project

BotC_yog is a behavioral analytics project built on Yogscast Blood on the Clocktower games. The pipeline automatically transcribes and diarizes each video, extracts game events, and builds structured datasets covering nominations, executions, player claims, and outcomes.

What it can do today: detect game phases & day structure · track player status and death events · extract nomination events · link nominations to execution episodes · attach claimed-role context to executions · flag lies (claimed role vs actual role card).

Current limits: nomination coverage is partial (not every vote window has a recoverable transcript signal) · vote tallies not yet extracted · night intent and rumor propagation not yet modeled.

🏰 Blood on the Clocktower

Blood on the Clocktower is a social deduction game for 5–20 players, played in person. Think Mafia or Werewolf, but with a dedicated Storyteller, full information during setup, and the ability to win even from the grave.

Players are split into two teams: Good (Townsfolk & Outsiders) and Evil (Minions & the Demon). Each player is dealt a secret role card with a unique ability. Good wins by executing the Demon; Evil wins if the Demon survives until only two players remain.

Every night the Demon and Minions wake silently to scheme, while Townsfolk may receive clues from the Storyteller. Every day, players openly debate, accuse, and vote to execute one suspect — then night falls again.

Role-claiming is central to strategy. Good players share information; Evil players lie about who they are. This project automatically transcribes every game and extracts behavioral data: who nominates whom, who gets executed, what roles players claim, and where those claims don't match their actual role card.

The Yogscast play on their charity streams, usually with 10–15 players. The games you see here have all been automatically transcribed and analysed for nominations, executions, claims, and social pressure.
